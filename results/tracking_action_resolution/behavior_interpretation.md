# Behavioral interpretation of the frozen action-resolution result

This is a post-Final explanation using only the already frozen action/speed/boundary/metric definitions and saved tables. It introduces no new metric, selected scene, intervention or threshold, and changes no frozen experiment source. Numeric tables can be regenerated by `python scripts/interpret_tracking_action_resolution.py` after the main analysis.

## The largest hit gain is a speed-1 phase-control effect

Medium is much more frequent at observable speed 1 than at speed 2, especially at d_pre=1. For Hybrid Final, medium occupies about 41.90% of speed-1 decisions versus 2.80% of speed-2 decisions. This is not simply a maximum-speed-matching effect.

The mechanics explain a useful local option: for a straight, speed-1 target one cell ahead on the same row, moving two cells reaches its next position after one target advance. Moving one cell stays one cell behind; moving three crosses farther ahead. This deterministic no-event example illustrates control authority, not privileged knowledge of the next stochastic event. In actual paired records, alignment opportunities and hits rise together, while firing given alignment remains 100%. A10 already counts many distance-1 states as close, so replacing distance 1 by exact alignment can markedly improve D/H with only a modest change in coarse C. Speed-2 slices improve too, but most of the aggregate hit gain comes from observable-speed-1 slices.

## Crossing current target position is not automatically a tracking error

The preregistered "overshoot" count is a geometric crossing of the *current* target coordinate before target motion. Its frequency **increases**, rather than decreases (Hybrid Final about +6.80 percentage points). That is consistent with anticipatory movement to the target's next location. It must not be advertised as a reduction in overshoot. Immediate command reversals decrease (Hybrid Final about -0.53pp), and Stay frequency falls from 13.65% to 2.10%; these are descriptive own-policy changes. Common-state action/value comparisons provide the separate controlled state comparison. Boundary/interior slices have policy-dependent cursor states and do not independently establish mechanism causality.

The fixed first-qualifying rules retain speed-2 medium disagreements **and regressions**. In Plan Final's first selected example (7200000, tick 17), A10's own post-distance is 3 and A14's is 4. No cherry-picked favorable replacement is used. Later route differences may reflect earlier decisions, so these fragments are not one-step counterfactual effects. Full real pre/post states are in `behavior_fragments.csv`.

## What is and is not established

Both terminals and all five Hybrid model pairs improve D, C and H on these new Final and Stress routes. A14 Final Plan/Hybrid reach C≥75% and preserve hits; Stress remains below 75%. The +5pp closeness joint gate remains unmet despite much more than +2% hits. Subset-value monotonicity passes over the complete physical state space at all depths, but that theorem does not establish rollout metric dominance. The favorable observed aggregate directions are empirical; saved regression fragments show the distinction.

A separately frozen fourteen-action learning experiment is justified next. It should distinguish whether an actually trained A14 policy can capture this phase-control benefit without full-state model-based planning, and should keep these current results as a completed paper section. No A14 Q-learning agent has been trained here.
